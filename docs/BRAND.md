# Strother Built Construction — Brand Guide

**Last updated:** 2026-09-16  
**Status:** Working direction for homepage concept; final brand system not yet client-approved

## 1. Brand Snapshot

**Brand name:** Strother Built Construction  
**Common abbreviation in existing mark:** SBC  
**Existing tagline:** “Make Your Dream Home a Reality” — legacy site copy; do not treat as approved for the redesign  
**New tagline:** None approved

### Brand in one sentence

A local residential remodeling contractor that should feel experienced, capable, trustworthy, and focused on meaningful home transformations rather than miscellaneous repair work.

### Core brand traits

- Craft-focused
- Trustworthy
- Established
- Local
- Confident
- Refined but approachable

### The website should feel

- Project-led
- Intentional
- Calm
- Substantial
- Credible
- Warm enough for a homeowner audience

### The website should not feel

- Like a handyman directory
- Like a generic contractor template
- Cheap or discount-driven
- Loud, cluttered, or keyword-stuffed
- Like a luxury real-estate brand disconnected from construction
- Like a tech/SaaS landing page
- Like a copy of Compelling Homes or Zenith Design + Build

## 3. Color System

The exact orange must be sampled from an approved logo/source asset before production. The following is a **working concept palette**, not a finalized brand standard.

| Role | Working name | Working value | Usage |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Brand accent | Strother Orange | `#F36C21` `[VERIFY]` | Primary CTA, small rules, selected labels, hover details |
| Primary dark | Charcoal | `#171717` | Headings, dark sections, footer |
| Primary light | Warm Ivory | `#F5F2EC` | Main background and editorial sections |
| Surface | White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards/forms where separation is useful |
| Secondary surface | Warm Stone | `#E9E3DA` | Alternating sections and subtle panels |
| Body text | Deep Gray | `#30302E` | Body copy |
| Muted text | Warm Gray | `#6F6A62` | Supporting copy and metadata |
| Border | Sand Line | `#D8D1C7` | Dividers, fields, restrained card edges |

### Color hierarchy

- Warm ivory, white, photography, and charcoal should dominate.
- Orange should occupy a small share of the visual field and signal action or identity.
- Use dark sections strategically for contrast; do not alternate dark/light mechanically.
- Prefer single-color surfaces over gradients.

### Avoid

- Large full-width orange sections resembling the current homepage
- Orange on black for long text
- Gold added solely to manufacture a “luxury” feel
- Purple/blue SaaS gradients
- Low-contrast beige-on-beige copy
- Colors sampled from reference companies

## 4. Typography

Typography must feel architectural and contemporary without becoming sterile.

### Working recommendation

**Display / headings:** Archivo  
**Weights:** 500–700  
**Use:** H1, H2, navigation emphasis, project titles

**Body / UI:** Source Sans 3  
**Weights:** 400–600  
**Use:** Body copy, buttons, labels, forms, supporting details

**Status:** Working mockup choice; verify repository availability/licensing and adjust if the logo or project imagery calls for a different pairing.

### Type personality

- Clean
- Structural
- Confident
- Highly readable
- Editorial through scale and spacing rather than ornamental type

### Type rules

- Use no more than two type families.
- Keep paragraphs relatively narrow and easy to scan.
- Use sentence case for most headings.
- Reserve uppercase for short labels or navigation—not paragraphs.
- Avoid ultra-thin text over imagery.
- Do not make every heading oversized.
- Verify “Strother Built Construction” and “Whole-Home Remodeling” at mobile widths.

## 5. Layout and Shape Language

### General composition

- Spacious editorial layout
- Strong full-bleed or edge-to-edge project photography where useful
- Clear grid and alignment
- Alternating image/text compositions without repeating the same component endlessly
- Large project moments balanced by concise copy
- Occasional asymmetry, but never at the expense of clarity

### Container

Use a consistent max-width appropriate to the repository, approximately 1200–1320px for primary content. Allow selected photography to break the content container intentionally.

### Shape treatment

- Square corners or subtle 2–6px radii
- Avoid bubbly cards and oversized rounded containers
- Use thin borders and whitespace before shadows
- Shadows should be rare and restrained

### Spacing

Airy but not empty. Each section should have enough space to establish hierarchy, while the page still feels active and grounded in real work.

### Icon style

Use simple line icons only when they improve comprehension. Do not rely on a grid of generic icons as the primary trust device.

## 6. Hero Direction

### Objective

Within the first few seconds, the visitor should understand that Strother is a Des Moines-area remodeling contractor focused on substantial residential transformations—not general handyman work.

### Preferred structure

- Minimal header with clear project-oriented navigation
- Optional eyebrow: “Home Remodeling in Greater Des Moines”
- Concise H1 focused on transformation or how the home is lived in
- One short supporting sentence naming the five project categories naturally
- Primary CTA: “Discuss Your Project” `[WORKING]`
- Secondary CTA: “View Our Work” `[WORKING]`
- One excellent completed-project image filling most of the viewport

### Working H1 directions

- “Built Around the Way You Live.”
- “Thoughtful Remodeling. Built to Last.”
- “Transform the Home You Already Love.”

These are concept directions, not approved copy.

### Hero imagery

**Priority:** A real, high-quality Strother kitchen, whole-home, or addition project  
**Fallback:** Licensed mockup image clearly marked for replacement  
**Focal point:** Finished craftsmanship and lived-in spatial quality, not tools or workers posing  
**Desktop:** Wide architectural crop with protected text area  
**Mobile:** Separate crop or object position preserving the room’s strongest detail

### Avoid

- Existing sketch/half-render hero treatment
- Logo centered over the main project image
- Generic construction tools, hard hats, tape measures, or stock crews
- Before/after sliders in the first viewport
- Busy image areas directly behind long copy
- Multiple competing CTAs

## 7. Photography and Imagery

### Priority order

1. Client-owned completed-project photography
2. Client-owned detail shots, before/after sets, team/site photos
3. Licensed placeholder imagery for the free concept
4. Abstract decorative imagery only if it supports the architecture

### Desired subject mix

- Finished kitchens
- Finished bathrooms
- Finished basements
- Whole-home transformations
- Additions/exterior integration
- Craft/detail closeups
- Optional process imagery showing organized, professional work

### Project presentation

Whenever possible, project cards or modules should include:

- Project type
- General location, if approved
- One concrete scope detail
- Multiple views or a strong before/after
- Link to a future case study

Do not invent project names, locations, budgets, or scope details.

### Image treatment

- Natural color with modest warmth
- Consistent exposure and crop logic
- No heavy orange overlays
- No fake depth-of-field or generative artifacts
- Avoid mixing obviously different stock-photo styles in one section

## 8. Components

### Buttons

**Primary:** Solid orange or charcoal fill, high-contrast label, modest corner radius  
**Secondary:** Text link or restrained outline  
**Hover/focus:** Clear contrast shift and visible focus ring; no dramatic movement  
**Copy style:** Action-oriented and specific

### Navigation

- Light or transparent over the hero only if contrast remains reliable
- Solid background after scroll only if the repository already supports it cleanly
- Keep primary links concise
- One visually dominant project CTA
- Mobile menu must expose all essential links without nested-hover dependence

### Service presentation

- Avoid eight identical thumbnail cards.
- Give the five primary services deliberate hierarchy.
- Use imagery and concise descriptions.
- It is acceptable for kitchen/whole-home/additions to receive larger visual treatment if the content supports it.

### Project presentation

- Large images
- Minimal text overlay
- Category/location metadata only when confirmed
- No fake case-study metrics

### Forms

- Clean, quiet, and easy to scan
- Labels remain visible
- Square/subtle-radius fields
- No production submission behavior during mockup phase

### Trust/proof

Prefer an editorial proof strip, quote, project story, or credential row. Avoid generic badge clouds or unsupported statistics.

## 9. Motion

- Subtle image scale or opacity transitions are acceptable if performant.
- Use gentle reveal timing only when it improves reading flow.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Do not use scroll hijacking, parallax that harms readability, long loaders, or elements flying in from every direction.
- The page should still feel complete with animation disabled.

## 10. Voice and Tone

### Voice

- Confident, not boastful
- Knowledgeable, not jargon-heavy
- Warm, not sentimental
- Direct, not keyword-stuffed
- Quality-oriented, not vague

### Writing patterns

Prefer:

- “Remodeling planned around how you live.”
- “Explore kitchens, bathrooms, basements, whole-home renovations, and additions.”
- “See the work.”
- “Discuss your project.”

Avoid:

- “We do it all.”
- “Your one-stop shop.”
- “Best contractor in Des Moines.”
- “Affordable luxury.”
- Repeating “Des Moines remodeling contractor” in every section
- Generic dream-home clichés
- Unsupported promises about timelines, budgets, or stress-free projects

Strother should feel more grounded and construction-forward than Compelling Homes, and less corporate/system-heavy than Zenith. The design should balance polished residential imagery with a straightforward local-contractor identity.

## 12. Responsive Notes

- Use a mobile-specific hero crop when necessary.
- Keep the primary CTA visible without forcing the entire desktop hero into one screen.
- Collapse trust indicators into a readable two-column or stacked layout.
- Avoid tiny project mosaics; prioritize one image at a time.
- Keep service names readable without truncation.
- Do not put essential project details only on image hover.
- Test navigation and long headings around 320px, 375px, 768px, 1024px, and large desktop widths.

## 13. Accessibility

- Maintain WCAG-friendly text contrast.
- Do not use orange alone to communicate state.
- Use real headings in logical order.
- Provide meaningful alt text based on each image’s purpose.
- Keep focus states obvious on light and dark surfaces.
- Ensure overlay text remains readable across responsive crops.
- Ensure animations and galleries work with keyboard and reduced-motion preferences.
